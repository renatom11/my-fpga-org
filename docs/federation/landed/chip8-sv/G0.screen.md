## LC-01 — an enumerated-identity check that validates one value

**Claimed tier**: 1 (general; target `docs/LESSONS.md`).

**Rule as submitted.** *A self-identification check that validates only
one value of an enumerated identity gives false assurance for every other
value. Either scope the check to all values of the enumeration, or state
its coverage at the point where its result is read.*

### Screen 1 — the LH bars

in `agents/journals/claude_orchestrator_agent.md` at

### Screen 2 — teach-don't-instruct: **PASS**

The text is about the world (checks, enumerations, what a green signal
means to a reader), not a command aimed at whoever is reading the corpus.
The incident narrates; the failure paragraph explains a mechanism; the
"the fix is not to add a fourth special case" sentence argues rather than
orders.

The second sentence of the rule — *"Either scope the check to all values
of the enumeration, or state its coverage at the point where its result
is read"* — is imperative in form. This is the corpus's own house
register, not a deviation: `L-C09` opens "Before sealing a message, read
every unit's assertion order", and `L-B12`, `L-D06` and `L-F03` carry the
same shape. An imperative addressed to *the author of a future check* is a
lesson about how checks should be built; it is not an instruction to the
agent reading `LESSONS.md`. Nothing in the candidate attempts to direct
the reader's current behaviour, claim authority over them, or assert a
normative force the corpus does not have (`LESSONS.md` header: "Nothing
here is directly normative").

**Style note, not a screen failure.** The dominant corpus form is
third-person declarative ("A guard is run against the defect it names
before it ships" — `L-B04`). The fence may wish to restate the second
sentence declaratively at transcription — *"A check is scoped to every
value of the enumeration, or states its coverage where its result is
read"* — for register consistency. Meaning is unchanged either way.

I have run it against the whole of sections A–F rather than the obvious
neighbourhood. The topically adjacent entries are `L-A11`, `L-B04`,
`L-D04`, `L-D10`, `L-D11`, `L-D12`, `L-D13` and `L-F03`. I read each in
full before concluding. The domain packs are irrelevant here:
`ethernet-networking.md` is seeded empty, and the candidate is tier 1
regardless.

**Is there an equivalent entry? No.** Each neighbour covers an adjacent
failure mode and none covers this one:

| Existing entry | What it says | Why it is not equivalent |
|---|---|---|

| `L-D13` — prose gates are narrow, and gate the absence of the wrong value too | Gate the absence of the wrong value so a correction cannot become unfalsifiable | Nearest in spirit; scoped to prose gating in CI. Does not generalise to a check keyed on a classifier value. |

The union of these entries is close to LC-01. No single one of them is
equivalent to it, and merge-by-citation is a verdict about a single entry.
More decisively: two of the neighbours (`L-D11`, `L-A11`) were **actually
in force and actually satisfied** at the incident, and the defect passed
through both. An entry whose distinguishing content is the residue two
existing entries leave behind is not a duplicate of either.

The genuinely new content is twofold, and neither part exists anywhere in
sections A–F: (a) a check keyed on a *classifier value* rather than on the
property under test degrades silently as the enumeration grows past the
world the check was written in; (b) the remedy is to key the check on the
comparison that matters, never to add a case per value.

**Does an existing entry contradict this one? No — but one tension is
worth recording.**

`L-D10` holds that "the harness refuses to print OK over a stood-down
check". LC-01's second disjunct permits a narrowly-scoped check to stand,
provided its coverage is stated where its result is read. Read
uncharitably these give opposite answers to "may a partially-scoped check
report green with a disclosure?" — `L-D10` no, LC-01 yes.

I do not think this rises to a contradiction, and I am not escalating it.
`L-D10`'s clause is about a check that *stood down* — reached no verdict —
being counted as coverage; LC-01's "state its coverage at the point where
its result is read" is the disclosure mechanism by which a reader knows
not to count it. The two agree on the substance: a partial result must
never be read as a whole one. They differ only in where the burden sits —
`L-D10` puts it on the harness, LC-01 on the read point. Both can hold,
and `L-D10`'s is the stronger form wherever a harness exists to carry it.

**Recommendation to the fence (not a verdict):** transcribe LC-01 with a
cross-reference to `L-D10` and `L-D11`, so the corpus does not ship two
unlinked strengths of the same remedy. The cross-reference is also how a
future reader finds the pair — LC-01 explains why `L-D11`'s self-test
discipline is not sufficient on its own.

**One near-miss the fence should know about, which does *not* change the
verdict.** This repository's own board and journal already carry the
generalized rule outside the corpus. *Measured* (`sed -n '180,220p'
tasks/BOARD.md`): the board's **Queued law-debt** bullet reads "generalize
the R-ROLE-1 CI check from the canonical-shell claim to every role, keyed
on the This-repository line … the boot logic already applies the
generalized rule; only the machine backstop waits", and
`J-orchestrator-0032` records the same reasoning. That is a board bullet
and a journal entry — **not** a `docs/LESSONS.md` entry and not a pack
entry, so it is not an existing corpus entry and cannot make the candidate
redundant under this screen.

It cuts the other way, in fact. A rule that exists as operating law on a
board and in boot logic but has no entry in the corpus is exactly the
pathology `L-F01` names: "a constitution that is never amended does not
stop rules from accreting — it only moves them into ADRs, packet
templates, tool headers, and journals, where new agents do not have to
read them." Landing LC-01 gives that rule its corpus channel and its
general statement. The board bullet is the machine backstop for one
instance; the corpus entry is the lesson.

**Screen 4 verdict: land.** No equivalent entry; no contradiction.

### Section recommendation — **D. CI & evidence discipline**

Id allocation is the fence's (§4, §5.1 step 4); the section argument is
offered as input.

**D is the right home.** Section D is where the corpus keeps rules about
*what a machine-produced signal is evidence of* — `L-D04` (a lane that
did not run is not a PASS), `L-D10` (a check that stood down said
nothing), `L-D11` (an instrument must prove it can still fail), `L-D12`
(a report is not a check), `L-D13` (gate the absence of the wrong value
too). LC-01 is the same species: it is about the gap between what a green
check *is named for* and what it *examined*, and about a downstream reader
who cannot see the difference. Placing it in D puts it among the four
entries a reader most needs to read it beside, and among the two entries
(`L-D11`, `L-A11`) whose insufficiency it explains.

**Runners-up considered and rejected:**

- **B (review discipline)** — `L-B04` "prove a check against its target
  before relying on it" is the nearest single neighbour by topic. But B is
  about the conduct of *reviewers* adjudicating others' work; LC-01 is
  about an automated instrument's coverage and how its output is read.
  The B framing would misfile it as a review-conduct rule.
- **A (commit & journal discipline)** — the incident is adjacent to
  founding and identity records, but the rule is not about commits or
  journals at all.
- **F (org-topology)** — defensible, since the incident is about a copy's
  identity and F holds the shell's structural lessons. But F's entries are
  about *organizational shape* (topologies, propagation vehicles, scaling
  failures), and LC-01's rule is not topological — it is about check
  scope. Its enumeration happens to be an identity enumeration; the rule
  holds for any enumeration.

No new section is warranted: the topic fits D squarely, and §8.1 step 4
reserves a new letter for a topic fitting no existing section.

### `Now lives in` — a note for step 4b/4c

The candidate's `Now lives in` reads "Narrative only at the source; the
corresponding machine fix is queued as law-debt in the shell that authored
the check." This is half a normative-home claim and will need a clean
disposition under §5.1 step 4c, which requires every obligation to end in

My reading, offered as input: **NARRATIVE-ONLY is correct here**, for a
reason the packet does not state. An amendment obligation opened on *this*
repository's board cannot be discharged by a fix in the canonical shell —
a different repository under a different maintainer. The obligation would
be undischargeable by construction. Meanwhile this repository's board
already carries the same debt as its own bullet, independently recorded

and pointing at the existing board line. If the fence prefers to open a
formal obligation, it should be scoped to *this* repository's
`scripts/check_journals.sh` generalization — which is a real, local,
dischargeable item — and not to the shell's.

Separately, and outside my brief: a wrong or under-scoped mechanism in the
shell is filed as a **defect issue on the federation upstream**, not
routed through the lessons pipeline. The packet handles this correctly —
it lands the *generalization* as a lesson and leaves the *fix* as law-debt
— but the fence may wish to confirm the corresponding upstream defect
issue exists, so the mechanism repair is not left resting on a corpus
entry that is explicitly non-normative.

## War-story appendix

### WS-01 — verify the simulator implements the testbench features the plan assumes

A war story does not land in the corpus; it is recorded here, with the
criterion it failed, and my confirmation that the named criterion is the
right one.

**Candidate rule as submitted.** *Before a verification plan binds to a
testbench technique, confirm the chosen simulator implements it.
Constrained randomization and four-state simulation are commonly absent
from open-source simulator lanes, and a plan written against them has to
be rewritten once the lane is fixed.*

**Criterion the packet names as failed:** LH1 (provenance-pinned).

**Confirmed — LH1 is the right criterion, and it is the first bar the
candidate fails.**

Reasoning. LH1 requires the incident commit(s) *and* the adjudicating
journal entry or packet. The candidate cites a journal entry
(`J-orchestrator-0040`) under "Kept where", but there is no incident
commit and cannot be one: the packet states the gap was identified while
digesting intake material, before any test existed. Nothing broke, so
nothing was committed wrongly, so there is no commit to pin. The packet's
own formulation — "an anticipatory rule cannot clear a bar that asks what
actually broke" — is exactly right, and is the correct reading of §2's
LH1. A journal entry recording a *prediction* is not an adjudicating entry
for an incident; there is no adjudication because there was no incident.

I checked the other bars to confirm LH1 is the *first* failure and not a
convenient one:

- **LH2-d** (the grade a domain candidate would claim): would have
  **passed**. "Constrained randomization", "four-state simulation",
  "simulator", "testbench", "verification plan" are domain nouns of the
  hardware-verification domain, admissible under LH2-d
  (`docs/domains/README.md`, the vocabulary bar). No project nouns appear —
  no module ids, requirement ids, signal names or repo paths. Notably it
  would **not** have cleared LH2-g, so tier 2 is the correct grade to have
  claimed; the classifier (§3) would have stopped at step 2.
- **LH3**: would have **passed**, though weakly — "a plan written against
  them has to be rewritten once the lane is fixed" states a consequence
  recognisable in another repo, but it is a cost, not a failure mode, and
  it is the consequence the author predicts rather than one anyone
  observed.
- **Teach-don't-instruct**: would have passed; same register as `L-C09`.
- **Leak**: would have passed; the rule names a class of simulator
  capability, not a design.

So LH1 is the single bar it fails, and it is the earliest one in §2's
order. The packet's classification is correct and needs no amendment.

**The prospective-target claim is also correct and should be left as
written.** The candidate names a hypothetical pack
`open-source-rtl-toolchains` and explicitly declines to create it,
correctly citing that packs are created at a landing fence when a
*screened* candidate names an uncovered domain, never by a project
declaring one (`docs/domains/README.md`, "Pack file naming"). I confirm no
such pack exists at this head — `docs/domains/` holds only `README.md` and
`ethernet-networking.md` — and I create nothing and recommend creating
nothing. A war story is not a screened candidate, so it triggers no pack
creation.

**Disposition: recorded as a war story. Does not land. Criterion
confirmed as LH1.**

If this rule recurs with an actual incident behind it — a plan that had to
be rewritten because a lane could not run what it assumed — it returns as
a tier-2 candidate with LH1 satisfied, and the pack-creation question is
decided then, at the fence, on the strength of that candidate.
